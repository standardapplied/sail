- **An incus test's setup says each step, and a step that crawls fails naming what it waited for.** On #529's run shard 1 was cancelled at its 20-minute limit with no word: `WorkspaceIT` printed nothing for 12.75 minutes between "Running" and its first test's output, where 17 to 25 s is usual. That runner was slow before any test ran (its JDK copy into the tool cache took 189 s against 3 s), and nothing said which setup step it was in. Every step of the shared base's setup (`AbstractIncusIT`: each launch, a spec box's pool, seal, install, stop, record and snapshot) and of `WorkspaceIT`'s, now prints a timestamped `[it-setup]` line when it starts and when it ends, with its bound and how long it took. Each command a step runs gets what is left of the step's bound as its timeout, so a step that outlasts its bound (a launch 120 s, a pool 180 s, the rest 120 s, against at most 18.9 s, 0.8 s and 2.5 s measured on real Incus) fails at once with `setup step '…' did not end within its bound of N s: it was waiting for \`…\``, on its `failed` line too, instead of the job being cancelled silent. A delete of a leftover that runs out of time now fails, naming it, where it was passed over.
- **A failed incus shard leaves what it needs to be read.** The test homes' logs (`target/it-home-*/.sail/logs`, where Sail records an incus start or stop that timed out) are now in the failed job's artifact: they sit under a hidden folder the upload passed over, so #529's shard 2 named a `containers.log` its artifact did not hold. And for any incus command a test reports as timed out (a launch, an exec, a delete, not only a start or a stop), the lane prints `incus info --show-log` of the container it named and the Incus daemon's last 200 journal lines, each asked within 30 s.
