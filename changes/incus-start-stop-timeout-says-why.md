- **A command Sail hands input to is timed, read and fed at once.** `ShellExecutor` wrote a command's whole input before it read the command's output or started its timeout, so a command that never read its input and never exited ran past its timeout for ever (one of 20 s with a 2 s timeout came back as exit 0), one that wrote back as it read deadlocked, and one that refused before reading came back as a broken pipe instead of its own refusal. Its output is now read and its input written on their own threads under one wait with the timeout, which kills the command's processes and ends the write; a command that ends without taking all its input answers with its own exit status, and one that exits 0 without taking it is raised as not having taken it, naming the command and never the input.
- **An `incus start` or `incus stop` that times out says why.** When a start or a stop of an instance Sail runs (a workspace's, a template's, a project's container) runs out of time, its error now says what the box said of the instance just after: systemd's state inside it and how many jobs it had queued, or that it no longer answered a command, and where the rest is (for example "Command timed out after 120s: incus stop acme. Just after, systemd in acme said it is stopping, with 2 jobs queued; Incus's log of acme and the rest are in /var/log/sail/containers.log."). The rest, Incus's log cut to its last 60 lines and the jobs' units, goes only to `/var/log/sail/containers.log` for root and `~/.sail/logs/containers.log` for anyone else (its folders made owner-only when missing), since the sentence can reach a node's other members; the whole diagnosis takes at most 10 s, Incus's log asked beside systemd, as it runs under the workspace's lock; the Incus integration lanes print and keep it when a job fails. It is the same diagnosis `sail init`'s timed-out steps use (`TimeoutDiagnosis`), one seam for both. Both logs are now made owner-only (0600) even when they already existed, and their directory is refused when its group or anyone else may write it. A symbolic link at the sail account's home is refused naming the bind mount that keeps the home on another disk, instead of "move it aside".
- **`sail agent attach` says a cold start before it starts.** An attach whose spec's workspace is stopped now prints "Starting <workspace>..." before the start, then its resuming or fresh line once the workspace is reached; an attach to the project's own container, or a resumed host session, says it only once the container is found running, never before a refusal. A word of why the pass did not reach a workspace that Incus refuses to store is now said once on the server's log, with Incus's reason.
