- **A signal goes only to the process its wrapper recorded.** Every signal Sail sends to a process it started in a workspace with no systemd unit holding it now first checks, inside the workspace, that the process at the pid file's pid has the start its wrapper recorded beside that pid file; otherwise nothing is signalled. That covers a chat's and a live room member's interrupt and stop, the halt of one left from before a restart, a foreground session's stop, and a claim of a spec's workspace that ends a person's terminal there. A pid file outlives its process, so after a restart of the box or the workspace its pid could name another of the person's processes, which the interrupt, the stop or the claim used to signal. One script holds the check for the single interrupt or stop and for the halt alike; a check that fails or answers nothing signals nothing and removes nothing.
- **A foreground session and a workspace's terminal record their start.** Their wrappers write the process's start beside its pid file (`agent.pid.start`, `~/.sail/terminal.pid.start`) before the pid, each step chained, so a record that cannot be written starts nothing. A foreground session or a terminal started by an earlier release has no record, so this release's stop or claim signals it not at all.
