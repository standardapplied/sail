- **The SailKit macOS lane keeps what will name a stall.** What held one of its runs for 20 s, with no test ending, is not known. The lane now prints the runner's image, cores and memory, keeps the suites' event stream in its `sailkit-macos-reports` artifact, and runs the suites under `scripts/stalls`, which ticks the machine's load every second and, only when no test has reported for 10 s while two or more run, keeps the processes using the CPU and a `sample` of every test process.
- **No terminal socket test passes or fails on how long its process was held**, once sail#547, which merges on top of this, holds the session-end limits of `SessionFixTests` and `OpeningEndTests` that it rewrites. A test's waits on a fake host, a session's follower, a held wait or a timer are waits for that event, ended only by the suite's time limit, which every socket suite now has; the fake host reads what a client sends on a thread of its own. A client limit a test is not about is `Patience.answer`, 120 s; a limit a test is about is a sleep the test holds and lets pass. A terminal session reads the time from the `now` it is given (the continuous clock unless a test hands its own), and the probe's `Runner` takes its steps' patience, the sleep that times their limits, and its close's limit: 5 s, 30 s for an attach and 10 s, as before. `FilesSession`'s quiet watchdog and stable-attachment check still read the wall clock; they follow in the spec's second change.
