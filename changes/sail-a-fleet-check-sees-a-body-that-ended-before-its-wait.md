- **A fleet check whose work ended before it began to wait passes at once.** `ci/fleet` waited for a check's work or its timer with `wait -n`, which can lose work that ended before the wait (as under `bash -c` or in a command substitution); such a check then sat out its whole limit (1200 s by default) and was reported as not ending within it. A check now waits for its own work, whose status bash keeps however early it ended, and asks the timer afterwards whether the limit came first; a check that outlasts its limit is still ended (TERM, then KILL after its grace) and fails naming the limit, and nothing of its timer outlives it, also when the run is stopped while the timer is being started.
- **ci/fleet refuses to start with SIGTERM ignored.** It stops its checks and their timers by TERM, so started where TERM is ignored a passing check would sit out its limit and fail; it now exits 2 at once and says so.
