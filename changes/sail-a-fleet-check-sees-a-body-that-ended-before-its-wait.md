- **A fleet check whose work ended before it began to wait passes at once.** `ci/fleet` waited for a check's work or its timer with `wait -n`, which loses work that ended before the wait once bash has dropped it from its job table, as `bash -c` does; such a check then sat out its whole limit (1200 s by default) and was reported as not ending within it. A check now waits for its own work, whose status bash keeps however early it ended, and asks the timer afterwards whether the limit came first; a check that outlasts its limit is still ended (TERM, then KILL after its grace) and fails naming the limit, and nothing of its timer outlives it. The wait no longer needs bash 5.1 (`wait -n -p`).
