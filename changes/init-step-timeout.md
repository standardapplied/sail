- **`sail init` creates the sail account's home empty, and a host step that times out says why.** On #500's terminal lane `sudo sail init` stopped after 120 s on its first host step, `id -u sail || useradd --system --create-home …`, with no reason. On the lane's 23 other runs that day the same step took 18–73 s, because `--create-home` copies `/etc/skel` and GitHub's Ubuntu runner installs Rust, nvm and dotnet tools there. The account only runs forced commands, so init now creates it with `--no-create-home`, and its next step makes `/home/sail` with `install -d -m 700 -o sail -g sail` in place of its `chmod 700`, which also sets an existing home's owner and mode. That step first refuses anything at `/home/sail` that is not a directory, a symbolic link included, naming what is there: `install -d` would follow a link and give its target to `sail`. The native fleet harness now makes its main's account from those same steps (`SshIdentityProvisioner.account()`), so the account its SSH logins reach is the product's. Separately, a command an init step runs that times out now carries `ps`'s listing of its processes, taken before they are killed (state, kernel wait channel, arguments); no other caller asks for it, so no other timed-out command pays for it. When an init host step times out, init also asks the box, just after, each question bounded to 5 s: `lslocks`, the account database's lock files, `systemctl is-active systemd-userdbd`, and `getent passwd sail`. It appends all of it to `/var/log/sail/init.log`, opened without following a link and created 0600 in a directory it refuses unless it is a real directory that root owns, and prints one sentence under the ✗ line, naming the process the step was stuck in, what it waited on, whether the account lookup answered and userdbd's state. The terminal lane prints that log when it fails.
